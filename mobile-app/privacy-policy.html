Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

Information We Collect

Obrium collects information you provide directly, including profile data, health information, and messages you send through the service.

How We Use Your Information

We use your information to provide personalized wellbeing advice. We do not sell your personal information.

Data Storage

Your profile, health answers, logged metrics and chat history are stored locally on your device. To generate Talk replies and Feed advice and to run automatic health checks, parts of this data are sent through Obrium's relay server to Google's Gemini API, as described below. Obrium's relay server does not store your messages or data: it passes each request to Gemini and returns the reply. If a request fails, the relay's error log may include the AI's reply text for troubleshooting. What Google does with the data is a separate matter, described in the next section.

AI Processing by Google Gemini

Obrium uses Google's Gemini API to write Talk's replies and Feed advice and to run automatic health checks. Each time Talk needs an AI response, Google receives the data below. Only fields you have actually provided are sent.

  • Conversation text: the message you send in Talk. Earlier messages in the chat are not included.
  • Profile: name, age, approximate location (city, ZIP code, or coordinates rounded to about 1 km — either typed by you or estimated as described under "Location and Weather Services"), stated goals, diet and habits, sex at birth, gender identity, height, weight, body build and blood type.
  • Health and wellbeing answers: health conditions, medications, supplements, allergies, last checkup, stress, mental energy, anxiety, mood changes and sleep patterns.
  • Biometrics and daily context: today's steps and sleep, your energy check-in, and local weather.
  • App context: the latest status lines from your Focus Area cards, recent Obrium prompts, and notes Obrium has inferred about you from how you use the app (you can read these in Settings).

Feed advice is a separate, smaller request. It is sent automatically when the app opens or you open Feed, after a daily check-in, and when the connection to Obrium's relay is restored. It includes only what you have provided of: your age, goals, diet and habits, and today's steps, sleep, glasses of water and energy check-in. It does not include your name, location, messages, health answers or inferred notes. Feed advice is on by default, and you can switch it off in Settings → Privacy & Security ("Generate advice on my Feed using AI"); when off, this request is not sent at all and the advice card is hidden.

Obrium currently uses the free (unpaid) tier of the Gemini API. Under Google's terms for unpaid use, Google may use what Obrium sends — including the profile, health and biometric data listed above — and Gemini's replies to provide, improve and develop Google's products and machine-learning technologies. Human reviewers at Google may read, annotate and process this input and output; Google says it first disconnects the data from Obrium's Google account, API key and project. Google's terms also advise against sending sensitive, confidential or personal information to the unpaid tier. If you do not want your data handled this way, do not use Talk, and switch off the automatic requests in Settings → Privacy & Security.

Location and Weather Services

These are independent services, not part of Google or Gemini. Each receives only what is listed here.

  • ipapi.co (operated by Kloudend, Inc.): if you have not entered a location in Settings, no location was saved in the last 7 days, and your device's precise location is unavailable (permission denied, not supported, or it fails), Obrium sends your IP address to ipapi.co to estimate your city. It is not called when you have entered a location or precise location works. The estimated city and approximate coordinates are saved as your location and may then be sent to Gemini as part of your profile, as described above. ipapi.co's own privacy policy applies to this request; it does not state a specific retention period for these lookups.
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim: when precise location works, your coordinates are sent to Nominatim to look up the name of your city.
  • Open-Meteo: your coordinates, or the city or ZIP code you entered, are sent to Open-Meteo to get local weather.
  • Optional Focus Area agents that you set up with your own API key or token (OpenWeatherMap, GitHub) contact those services directly.

Automatic Analysis

Some requests happen automatically, without you sending a message. When you open Talk while profile questions are still unanswered, Obrium asks the next question from a fixed script on this device; that opening is not sent to Gemini. Gemini is asked to phrase the question only when that field has no scripted wording. When new steps or sleep values are logged (for example, when you mention them in Talk), Obrium sends only those values (date, steps, sleep) to Gemini to check for a significant shortfall; if it finds one, a short "Worth a look" note is shown once the next time you open Talk. Both automatic behaviors are on by default, and you can switch each off in Settings → Privacy & Security; when profile questions are off, Talk does not ask them on its own, and when health checks are off, that request is not sent at all. Feed advice (described above) is also automatic and has its own switch in the same place.

Your Rights

What Obrium stores is kept on this device, and you can see and remove it there:

  • Profile and health answers: view and change them in Settings → Edit profile data.
  • Chat history: Talk → ⋯ → Clear chat.
  • Notes Obrium has inferred about you: Settings → Notes Obrium has inferred about you → Clear these notes.
  • Everything, all at once: Settings → Delete all local data. This is currently the only way to remove logged steps and sleep, daily check-ins, settings, and the on-device record of what was sent to the AI.

Deleting data on this device does not recall data already sent to Google's Gemini API; Google handles that data under its Gemini API terms, as described above.
